Speech-to-text with Nigerian-accented English

Pilot · October 2026 · Uchechukwu Ekpendu

Before a larger study on speech input for generative AI, I wanted to know how well two common speech recognisers handle Nigerian-accented English on short "edit this page" instructions.

What I did

Three Nigerian English speakers, from two different ethnic groups, each read the same 10 short instructions, the kind you'd give an AI to edit a web page (30 recordings, 234 words in total). Chrome's built-in speech recogniser (language set to en-NG) transcribed each one live. OpenAI's Whisper (small.en, run locally) transcribed the saved audio afterwards. I scored both with word error rate (WER): the share of words that were wrong, missing or added, after removing punctuation and capitals.

Results

SpeakerChrome WERWhisper WER
S1-Potato5.1%7.7%
S2-Prime3.8%12.8%
S3-Zenin6.4%28.2%
All (30 recordings)5.1%16.2%

Perfect transcripts: Chrome 18 of 30, Whisper 16 of 30. Lower WER is better.

  • Chrome did better overall: about 1 wrong word in 20, against 1 in 6 for Whisper.
  • Whisper depended a lot on the speaker: from 7.7% to 28.2%. Chrome stayed between 3.8% and 6.4%.
  • The error rate alone hides what matters. Some errors are harmless ("accent" for "accents"). Others change the edit completely: Chrome heard "bolder" as "border" or "boulder" for all three speakers, and "border" is a real design instruction.

Limitations

  • Three speakers from only two ethnic groups is tiny, and there's no comparison accent yet. Treat these numbers as a first look, not a finding.
  • People read a script in a quiet room. Real prompting is spontaneous and messier.
  • Chrome heard live audio while Whisper got the saved file, and I tested only one Whisper size. Larger models may do better.
  • Chrome's recogniser is a cloud service and can change without notice.

Implications and future work

Speech input works for Nigerian-accented English, but which engine you pick matters, and so does who is speaking. Counting wrong words isn't enough either. What matters is whether an error changes what the AI does.

Next, I'm extending this to about 12 speakers across Yoruba, Igbo and Hausa backgrounds, and marking which errors change the instruction. Longer term, I want to compare Nigerian and North American English on real generative AI prompts, see how recognition errors change the way people prompt, and test interface fixes, like confirming uncertain words before the AI acts.
